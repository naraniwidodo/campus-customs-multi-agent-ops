Part 1 — How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

The performance of the agent, in my opinion, is relatively good, although I faced a problem in my Problem 9 (as can be seen through the back-and-forth prompts and bugs that I tried to fix).

Ticket 101 — the work stopped after human approval

The biggest problem was how in Ticket 101 the agent did not continue the work after a task was marked for human approval — meaning that after the human's approval the task stopped, the Boss did not pick the approval up, and the ticket could never be resolved. I vibe-coded an explanation that the agents should pick the job up after human approval, which is the only way the task is resolved.

The reason is that I did not write in detail when I vibe-coded the looping mechanism after human approval. On reflection, I think there was a confusion about post-approval: the agent prepares materials to approve and the human approves — but who pays, the AI or the human? I brought this confusion to a TA via chat and got a clarification that let me continue the work.

Ticket 101 — the customer email was too technical

Another problem in 101 was that the Customer Service agent drafted an email to the customer, Tauhid, that was very technical — something like "the finance team has not made the payment, so no stock yet" — which I think is not proper for a customer.

Luckily, Customer Service only drafted the email and did not send it, as prompted, so I got the chance to review it and vibe-code the improvements: hide all technical information, focus on a yes/no answer, and only give a final response after all tasks have been completed.

The safety rule earned its keep here. "Nothing is ever sent" was what turned a bad message into a reviewable draft rather than something a customer received.

Ticket 102 — refused to overspend

For 102 the agent did the work very well: it refused to make any payment because there was no cash balance after I had worked 101 and 103, exactly as prompted. When I reset and did 102 first — so there was enough cash — the agent also worked pretty well, although the task is relatively uncomplicated because the payment is not due yet.

Ticket 103 — benefited from the 101 fix

For 103, I suspect that if I had not vibe-coded and fixed the bug in 101, then 103 would have had the same problem — the task stopping after human approval. But it did not. 103 gives approval on both the discount and the order, which I think is the right way.

Part 4 — Three new problems this agent team could solve with the tools built

  1. Vendor dependency and outstanding obligations
    Accounting · get_financial_info, get_vendor_info

    Our agents can help assess a vendor's financial relationship with Campus Customs, including outstanding payables and the total value of our transactions with that vendor — identifying vendors Campus Customs depends on heavily. This helps us understand the scale of the business relationship with each vendor and identify outstanding financial obligations, supporting human decision-making.

  2. Products tying up too much cash
    Inventory, Accounting · get_product_info, get_financial_info

    Our agents can help assess products with high inventory investment, reviewing product information against available financial data. This matters to prevent short cash flow and keep cash moving rather than sitting idle in the warehouse.

  3. Products becoming less profitable
    Accounting · get_product_info, get_financial_info

    Reflecting on ticket 103, which included a discount, our agents can help identify products that may be becoming less profitable — reviewing product details against available financial records to surface concerns about product costs and profitability. This helps Campus Customs decide which products need further review and make better-informed business decisions.

Part 5 — Three problems this team could not solve, and what would be needed

1. Customer complaints and non-product questions

I have not coded anything to support either, and the database to work on this definitely needs to be developed.

Agents needed: more agents — for example a Logistics agent for shipping complaints, alongside the existing Customer Service — supplied with a database holding information on possible complaints and non-product questions.

Tools needed:

  • get_order_status
  • track_shipment
  • get_complaint_history
  • search_faq_knowledge_base
  • create_complaint_ticket
  • update_complaint_status
  • get_resolution_options
  • draft_customer_response

We should also make sure Customer Service gives a proper response per complaint or question — reflecting the ticket 101 lesson in Part 1, where the agent gave a very technical response to a customer.

2. Negotiation on rent, lease, vendor and other payments

With our current tools the Accounting agent can only look at the invoice and prepare materials for human approval. It cannot yet help with negotiation, or with the data analysis that would help a human make that approval.

Agents needed: possibly a new Price & Market Comparison agent, or additional tools under Accounting.

Tools needed:

  • categorize_expenses
  • analyze_historical_expenses
  • compare_vendor_pricing
  • compare_expenses_with_market_rates
  • identify_cost_saving_opportunities
  • forecast_future_expenses
  • calculate_cost_impact
  • draft_vendor_negotiation_proposal

We might also need to connect the agents to external sources of data and analysis, such as Yahoo Finance.

Note on safety rule 3. External data sources would be the first break from "no private or external sources". Today no agent has network access at all — the only toolset is a local server over one database. That boundary would need rewriting deliberately, not incidentally.

3. Hidden or suspicious fees

I am not sure whether the Accounting agent can spot hidden fees right now. I think it could flag them when it prepares materials for human payment review, but since we have not vibe-coded anything or added a tool here, I would add it.

Tool needed: detect_unexpected_charges — compare invoice line items against contract terms and expected fees, to flag unexplained charges or potential hidden fees for human review before payment approval.

It cannot today, and the reason is structural. The invoices table holds a single amount and a free-text description — there are no line items to compare, and no contract terms stored anywhere. This needs new tables before it can need a new tool.
